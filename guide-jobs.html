Jobs you can get

Common first jobs for political science majors, what they ask for and what they pay. Part of Jobs and careers, for graduate school and law school.

Few political science majors become "political scientists": that title usually means a PhD (BLS). Most work in government, law, nonprofits, media, research and business. Here are common first jobs, what employers ask for and what they pay in New York.

JobWhat you'd doWhat they ask forPay
Legislative aideSearch for: Legislative Aide, Staff Assistant, Legislative CorrespondentAnswer constituents, track bills, write letters and memos, and keep an elected official's office running.A bachelor's (political science is often named), strong writing, and often a year of office, campaign or internship work. City Council members hire on their own, so contact their offices. Start with: Senate or Assembly session programs, City Council and congressional internships.$50,000–55,000 in a New York State senator's office (2026 posting). House staff assistants: $60,007 median, $45,000 minimum (Congressional Research Service, 2025).
Research assistant or policy associateSearch for: Research Assistant, Research Associate, Staff Associate, Policy AssociateFind and clean data, write literature reviews and briefs, and support senior researchers at a think tank, university or nonprofit.A social science degree, statistics and data cleaning; R, Stata or Python helps (see Methods and skills). Start with: Brennan Center, Vera, Center for an Urban Future.$66,300–69,000 for an entry-level staff associate at Columbia's Incite Institute (2026 posting). New York-area median: $65,330 (BLS).
ParalegalSearch for: Paralegal, Legal Assistant, Paralegal AideOrganize case files, draft and file court papers, and track deadlines for lawyers. A common job before law school.Many employers take a bachelor's in any subject; some want a paralegal certificate. The city's Civilian Complaint Review Board also hires investigators with a bachelor's and strong writing ($49,148 to start). Start with: working as a paralegal, the Legal Studies minor.$50,198–70,247 for a NYC Law Department paralegal aide (2026 posting). New York-area median: $75,330 (BLS).
OrganizerSearch for: Field Organizer, Community Organizer, Tenant OrganizerRecruit and train volunteers, knock on doors, and build tenant or neighborhood groups around a campaign.Organizing or volunteer experience, often a year; evenings and weekends; a second language such as Spanish helps. Campaigns hire most in the spring and summer of election years, often for a few months. Start with: NYPIRG, Emgage, a local campaign.$50,480–56,089 for a Bronx housing organizer (2026 posting). Campaign field jobs often pay by the month: $3,000–4,500 on a 2026 State Senate campaign.
Communications or press assistantSearch for: Communications Assistant, Press Assistant, Digital Media CoordinatorDraft press releases and social media posts, keep media lists, track news coverage and support the spokesperson.Clear, fast writing, social media and Microsoft Office, plus writing samples. Start with: the journalism minor, journalism programs.$78,000 for the ACLU's entry-level communications fellowship; $47,500–52,500 for a State Senate digital media job (2026 postings). New York-area median: $84,130 (BLS).
Data analystSearch for: Data Analyst, Research Analyst, Data Visualization AnalystClean and analyze program data, and build reports and dashboards for a nonprofit or agency.Excel plus R, Python or SQL, and a tool like Tableau or Power BI; a year or two of data work is often preferred. Start with: the Commonpoint Data & Insights Fellowship, CUNY Tech Prep, a free Google Data Analytics certificate (Job search tools).$60,000–65,000 at Coalition for the Homeless; $72,000 for a data visualization analyst at the Bronx District Attorney's office (2026 postings).
New York City governmentSearch for: Assistant Analyst, Community Associate, Community Coordinator, Staff AnalystAnalyst, program, outreach and planning jobs in city agencies, from the budget office to City Planning.Some take just a bachelor's, like the budget office's Assistant Analyst. Community Coordinator and Staff Analyst want a bachelor's plus 2 years, or a master's. Permanent jobs usually need a civil service exam. Start with: Urban Fellows, NYC Civic Corps, the Public Service Corps.Starting pay in 2026 postings: Assistant Analyst $60,764–77,327; Community Coordinator $62,868; Staff Analyst $62,517; Community Associate $44,545; City Planning research assistant $52,791–79,882. All on NYC Jobs.
Federal governmentSearch for: Management Analyst, Political Analyst, and the "Recent graduates" filter on USAJOBSProgram, management and intelligence analysis in federal agencies, many in Washington.Pathways jobs for recent graduates take you within 2 years of your degree (OPM). Good grades (about a 3.0 overall or 3.5 in your major) can qualify you to start at GS-7. Intelligence jobs need US citizenship and a polygraph, and usually a move to Washington. Start with: CIA, State Department, Partnership for Public Service.In New York, GS-7 starts at $59,465 and GS-9 at $72,737 (OPM, 2026). The CIA's political analyst job runs from GS-8 to GS-15.
ReporterSearch for: Reporter, News Assistant, Editorial AssistantReport and write the news, from City Hall and Albany to your neighborhood.Clips: two or more published stories. Knowing local politics helps; the student paper and internships count. Start with: the journalism minor, POLITICO, the Newmark 4+1.$66,500–72,000 for an early-career City Hall reporter at City & State (2026 posting). National median: $62,200 (BLS).
TeacherSearch for: NYC Teaching Fellows, Teach For America, Social Studies TeacherTeach middle or high school.NYC Teaching Fellows has no social studies track for 2027, but its special education tracks take a bachelor's in any subject (2.5 GPA). Teaching social studies takes a teacher preparation program. Start with: Teach For America, City Year.NYC public schools start at $71,314 with a bachelor's and $80,166 with a master's (NYC Public Schools, 2026).
Compliance associateSearch for: Compliance Associate, Compliance AnalystRun background and conflict-of-interest checks, review filings and make sure an organization follows the rules.A bachelor's, careful reading and Excel. Good preparation for law school.$75,000 at the NYC Council (2026 posting). New York-area median: $101,050 (BLS).
Consulting analystSearch for: Business Analyst, Government and Public Services AnalystResearch a client's problem, build models and slide decks, often for government agencies.Big firms recruit a year ahead, in the fall of senior year, and set GPA cutoffs (3.2 at Deloitte). Government work can mean a security clearance and a lot of travel.$88,000–91,000 for a Deloitte government analyst (2026 posting).
Government affairsSearch for: Government Affairs Associate, Public Affairs AssociateResearch policy for clients, draft memos and talking points, track bills and pitch reporters.Usually 1–3 years in government, often as legislative staff, so it is more often a second job. Research, writing and trips to Albany.$60,000–70,000 for an associate at a public affairs firm (2026 posting).
Survey or market research analystSearch for: Research Analyst (polling or market research)Write questionnaires, run surveys, analyze the results and present them.A bachelor's in an analytical field (liberal arts counts), statistics, Excel and PowerPoint; Qualtrics helps.$55,500–65,500 for an Ipsos research analyst in New York (2026 posting). National median: $78,760 (BLS).

Pay comes from job postings in New York in 2026 and from the Bureau of Labor Statistics. BLS medians cover every experience level (New York area), so first-year pay is usually lower. Postings close; search the titles above for current ones.

Where to look

  • HireQC (Handshake). Employers looking for Queens College students.
  • NYC Jobs and StateJobsNY. City and state government. Check the civil service exam schedule early; many permanent city jobs need one.
  • USAJOBS. Federal jobs. Filter by "Recent graduates" in the two years after you finish.
  • City & State Jobs. New York politics: legislative offices, campaigns, advocacy and government affairs.
  • Idealist. Nonprofit jobs and fellowships. More tools in Job search tools.

At Queens College

  • Center for Career Engagement and Internships. Résumé reviews, mock interviews, career fairs and the FOCUS 2 career assessment. Frese Hall 213; book a career educator on QC Navigate.
  • Political Science internships. Earn credit for an internship, including full-time spring semesters in Albany with a stipend.
  • The department's brochure. Its "What kind of jobs can I get" section lists where majors go.
  • APSA's student resources. Careers in law, government, campaigns, journalism, nonprofits, business and teaching.
Get experience while you're in school.

Almost every posting above asks for writing, data skills or a year of related work. An internship, a campus job or a research job with a professor counts. Not sure which job fits you? Try the prompts in Using AI the right way, then check what it says with the career center.
