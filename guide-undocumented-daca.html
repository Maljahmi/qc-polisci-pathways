Undocumented and DACA

Help at QC and CUNY, paying for school, work and the bar. Part of Support and your situation, for graduate school and law school.

Undocumented students and DACA recipients go on to graduate school, law school and good jobs, and many programs on this site are open to them. Here is where to get help at Queens College and CUNY, how to pay for school and what the rules are now.

Help at Queens College and CUNY

  • Immigrant Student Support Initiative. Queens College's office for immigrant students "regardless of their immigration status." King Hall 213, 718-997-3990, ImmigrantSupport@qc.cuny.edu; book through QC Navigate (care unit "Student Affairs," tag "Immigrant Support"). Its pages cover money, scholarships, work and legal help. Immigrant Student Support
  • CUNY Citizenship Now! Free immigration legal help for any New Yorker: citizenship, DACA renewals, TPS and fee waivers. The Queens College center is in the Student Union, Room 100, by appointment (646-347-4774), or call 646-664-9400. Citizenship Now!
  • Quick legal questions. The CUNY Immigration Assistance Project offers short consultations with an attorney for current CUNY students. New York State's Office for New Americans has a hotline: 1-800-566-7636.

DACA right now

U.S. Citizenship and Immigration Services still accepts and processes DACA renewals and work permits, and current grants stay valid until they expire, but it "will not process initial DACA requests at this time." Its page was last updated in January 2025. Talk to Citizenship Now! before you file anything. uscis.gov/DACA

Paying for school

  • Federal aid. Undocumented students and DACA recipients can't get federal grants or loans, so state aid and private scholarships matter more.
  • The New York State DREAM Act. Opens up state aid like TAP and the Excelsior Scholarship if you went to a New York high school for two or more years and graduated, earned a New York high school equivalency diploma, or pay in-state tuition at CUNY or SUNY (with time limits after your diploma). It doesn't ask applicants without status for a home address or financial records, and you only establish eligibility once. NYS DREAM Act
  • Scholarships. TheDream.US (up to $33,000 for college); the Paul & Daisy Soros Fellowships for graduate school, which accept DACA recipients (up to $25,000 a year plus half of tuition; due October 29, 2026); and the Hispanic Scholarship Fund, which accepts DACA recipients and graduate students.
  • Graduate school loans. TheDream.US runs graduate loan programs, including a private loan open to graduates with DACA or TPS.
  • Lists to check. Immigrants Rising's scholarship list (no proof of citizenship needed) and My Undocumented Life's graduate school funding. On this site, see every program open to DACA students.

Working

  • Without work authorization. You can still earn money as an independent contractor or by starting a business. An ITIN lets you file taxes and get paid as a contractor, though it does not authorize work or give you status. See QC's work opportunities page and Immigrants Rising.
  • Paid projects. TheDream.US scholars at QC can do paid micro-internships through Parker Dewey, about $20 an hour and up to $4,000 a year, with an SSN or ITIN and a 2.5 GPA. QC's internship stipend is open to undocumented students with ITINs.

Law school and the bar

  • New York admits DACA recipients as lawyers. In Matter of Vargas (2015), a New York appeals court held that "undocumented immigrants who have been granted DACA relief... may be admitted to the practice of law" if they meet the other standards. The decision doesn't address applicants without DACA.
  • LSAT fee waivers. DACA recipients and applicants, TPS holders and affirmative asylum seekers can ask LSAC for a fee waiver. See LSAT and fees.
  • A guide to read. Immigrants Rising's Law School Resource Guide (June 2024). Its Pre-Law Fund ended in 2025.

Rules for undocumented and DACA students change often. Check with the Immigrant Student Support Initiative or Citizenship Now! before you apply or file anything.
