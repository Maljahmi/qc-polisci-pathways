Getting involved at Queens College

Clubs, student government, leadership and honor societies. Part of Get ready, for graduate school and law school.

Clubs, student government and campus leadership roles are where you meet the students and professors who become your network, and they give you something real to write about in applications. Pick one or two and stick with them.

Clubs

  • Find one. More than 150 QC clubs are on UKnighted; club pages need your CUNY login.
  • For political science majors. Model United Nations goes to two National Model UN conferences a year, and delegates chosen for the Washington conference go free; there's also a 3-credit Model UN course, PSCI 297. The QC Pre-Law Society, Mock Trial and Women in Law are for students headed to law school (see QC and CUNY pre-law resources). The Speech & Debate Society, the History Club, the Philosophy Club, UNICEF at QC and The Knight News, the student newspaper, are good fits too.
  • Start your own. You need ten QC students: six members and four officers with at least a 2.0 GPA. Register it on UKnighted. Clubs and organizations

Student government

  • Student Association. QC's student government, the link between students and the administration. Elections are each April; the 2026 vote ran April 19–25. Student elections
  • Academic Senate. Twenty student senators serve one-year terms alongside faculty on the body that sets QC's academic policies. Academic Senate

Leadership and service

  • Leadership roles. The Office of Student Development and Leadership runs the Knight's Round Table Leadership Series and picks orientation leaders, Junior Marshals and UBUNTU mentors. Student Union LL23, 718-997-3970, Studentlife@qc.cuny.edu. Leadership
  • Volunteering. Hunger and Homelessness Awareness Week in November, Alternative Spring Break and the Knights Day of Service; email volunteer@qc.cuny.edu. Volunteer
  • Civic work. Poll work, voter registration and internships with elected officials are in Working in politics and campaigns.

Honors

  • Pi Sigma Alpha. The national political science honor society. QC's chapter, Beta Kappa, is on the national list, but the department's pages don't mention it, so ask the department whether it is inducting members. You need at least half the credits for your degree, 10 credits of political science with a B average or better, and an overall GPA in the top third of your class; there's a one-time $35 fee. Members can apply for $2,000 Washington internship scholarships, win paper awards and present at the national student conference. Membership
  • Honors you can still join. Honors in the Social Sciences, the Honors in the Humanities minor (a 3.3 GPA; you can start as a sophomore) and the Business and Liberal Arts honors minor (a 3.0 GPA).
  • Macaulay Honors College. Only incoming freshmen and students at CUNY community colleges can apply, so current QC students can't join. Macaulay admissions
