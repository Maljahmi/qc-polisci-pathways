Building a network

Professors, the career center and NYC events. Part of Jobs and careers, for graduate school and law school.

Many jobs and internships come through people you know, and you're in New York City, which has more networking events than you could ever go to.

  • Ask your professors. Tell them the field you're curious about, and ask whether they know anyone you could talk to.
  • Use the career center. The Center for Career Engagement and Internships runs workshops and events and a career and internship fair where employers recruit.
  • Go to events. Look for talks, panels and mixers at CUNY, nonprofits, think tanks and city agencies; many are free. Introduce yourself, ask one good question, and follow up by email.

Career fairs

  • At Queens College. The social services and nonprofit fair is October 27, 2026, and the spring career and internship fair is in March. Sign up on HireQC (Handshake).
  • Elsewhere in CUNY. The College of Staten Island's spring fair welcomes every CUNY student, and the CUNY LEADS fair at Hunter is for students with disabilities.
  • Government jobs. The city's Civil Service 101 session (online, November 17), monthly Jobs NYC hiring halls in every borough, and the New York Public Library's government career fair each June.
  • Museums. The STEM2CITY fair at the American Museum of Natural History brought more than 15 city agencies, from the City Council to the MTA, to meet people 16 to 24 in March 2026, and the New York Historical holds a museums and culture internship fair in the fall.

Graduate and law school fairs

  • In the city. The CUNY Graduate School Fair (October 21, 2026), the Idealist fair each September, Baruch's Marxe School open houses and the LSAC Law School Forum each October.
  • Online. PPIA's fair (October 22), APSIA's (October 29) and LSAC's digital forums (November 18).

Free talks and young professional groups

  • Talks. Roosevelt House at Hunter, the CUNY Graduate Center and the Foreign Policy Association hold free evening talks. Stay after and introduce yourself to a speaker or the person next to you.
  • Groups. ABNY Young Professionals (free civic events with city leaders) and Young Professionals in Foreign Policy (free for students the first year).
  • Everything in one list. See every event and fair, and add the ones you like to your calendar.

This is all general advice, so don't feel pressured. I know it's overwhelming. But if you put in your best effort and explore what you really like, you can find a job in that sector, whatever you want it to be, or get into law school or graduate school. Just take your time and put effort into your classwork, your applications and networking. Don't be afraid: people are usually nice and will help you. You just have to make it easy for them!
