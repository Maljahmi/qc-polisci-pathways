Politics and campaigns

Campaign jobs, party internships, poll work and New York election dates. Part of Jobs and careers, for graduate school and law school.

Campaigns, parties and voter groups take on students every year, and New York has an election every November. You can start as a volunteer, a poll worker or an intern, whatever your party, or if you don't have one.

What campaign staff do

A common first campaign job is field organizer. In the New York State Democratic Committee's 2026 posting, organizers recruited and trained volunteers, knocked on doors, ran phone banks and community events, and tracked daily goals for reaching voters. No experience was needed, but you needed a phone and a car and had to be ready to move, and it paid $5,525 a month. The Republican National Committee trains organizers through its Republican Leadership Initiative.

When campaigns hire

Campaign jobs last for one election, so they're posted in the months before it and end soon after. Any campaign will take volunteers, which is a good way to meet the staff. For House races, the parties collect résumés: the NRCC's campaign jobs bank for Republicans and the New York State Democratic Committee's jobs page for Democrats in New York.

New York's next elections

State law puts primaries on the fourth Tuesday in June and general elections on the Tuesday after the first Monday in November. That makes the next ones June 22 and November 2, 2027, then June 27 and November 7, 2028; 2028 also has a presidential primary on a date not set yet. Early voting runs from ten days to two days before each election. Confirm dates with the State Board of Elections, and see the city Board of Elections' guide to New York City elections.

Paid election work in New York City

  • Poll worker. The city Board of Elections pays election inspectors $250 a day plus $100 for passing the training, and interpreters up to $250 a day plus $25; you can earn up to $2,750 for ten days. Inspectors must be registered Democrats or Republicans. Interpreters need to be 18, live in the city and be citizens or permanent residents, and Queens needs Chinese, Korean, Hindi and Bengali speakers as well as Spanish. Poll worker jobs
  • NYC Votes. The city's nonpartisan Campaign Finance Board hires part-time community education interns to run voter workshops and registration drives, at $17.85 to $21.40 an hour.
  • Elected officials. Internships with the City Council, the State Senate and Assembly in Albany and Congress, and CUNY's Rogowsky program, which includes a Model State Senate in Albany for students QC nominates.
  • For credit. PSCI 294 is a full semester in Albany with a stipend, and PSCI 2925 is an urban politics internship. Political science internships

Party and nonpartisan internships

Party committees want interns from their own party: the DNC expects interns to be registered Democrats, and the RNC asks for registered Republicans. Nonpartisan groups don't ask.

  • Democrats. The DNC pays $3,700 a term (spring is due November 27), and the DCCC, which works to elect House Democrats, pays a stipend.
  • Republicans. The RNC's summer Eisenhower program pays a stipend, and the NRCC works to elect House Republicans. The conservative Leadership Institute pays $1,000 a month with free housing (spring is due December 1).
  • Nonpartisan and bipartisan. The Bipartisan Policy Center ($6,000 for a summer), the League of Women Voters of the City of New York (unpaid, in Manhattan), IGNITE for young women, Running Start and NYPIRG.
