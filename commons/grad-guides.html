Start here

This path covers funded PhDs and policy master's for any Queens College Political Science major who is even curious about grad school. You don't need to be sure yet. Most of these programs exist to help you find out.

Three ground rules

  • Good research programs pay you. Almost everything here is free and comes with a stipend, housing, or both. Be skeptical of programs that charge you to "do research."
  • Deadlines move every year. Confirm on the official page before you plan around a date.
  • Apply to more than one. These programs are competitive, and a rejection says little about you.
  • Take the methods courses early. PSCI 200 (Research Skills) and an intro statistics course such as PSYCH 107 or DATA 205. Either one also counts toward the Data Analytics minor.
  • Ask for research assistant work. No office posts it. Pick one or two professors whose work you understand, and email what you read, what skill you bring and how many hours you have.

Get credit for it. QC's political science internship courses give credit for urban politics, law and advocacy, and international politics placements. CUNY's Rogowsky Internship Program places juniors and seniors in government for 3–12 credits.

Undocumented or an international student? Emory SOAR says it accepts undocumented and international students. MIT MSRP accepts international students with a visa and work authorization. RBSI, MIT and Michigan also take DACA recipients.

Statements, help and AI

Pick programs that fit, and get help with your statement. Read what each program focuses on and make sure it matches what you want to study. Then ask your professors and Rebecca Baron, QC's Advisor for Scholarships and Fellowships in the Office of Honors and Scholarships, to help with your statements of purpose. They are here to help. Don't lose out on an opportunity because you were scared to ask. You're in college to learn, so learn.

Don't let AI write your application. AI is useful for some tasks, like building this website. But professors read the applications to these programs, and they will know if AI wrote yours. Use it as a proofreader instead: give it the prompt and your own draft, and ask where you could improve or show more of yourself. It's a machine, and it doesn't know you better than you know yourself. Before you submit, have peers and professors read your writing too.

Talk to your professors

Your research doesn't have to be political science. Methods travel across fields, and CUNY faculty in many departments need student help with surveys, data, experiments, fieldwork and archives.

What research looks like next door

  • Sociology. Surveys and interviews on inequality, immigration, work and neighborhoods. Home of QC's data analytics program.
  • Economics. Labor markets and policy evaluation, often with large administrative datasets and causal inference.
  • Psychology. Experiments on attitudes, prejudice and decision-making, the base of political psychology.
  • Urban studies. Housing, transit, policing and city budgets, often with NYC open data.
  • Anthropology and history. Fieldwork, oral histories and archives.
  • Linguistics. Text as data, and language and identity (see Pair your major).

Going to office hours

Office hours are not only for grade questions. Professors expect students to come in and ask for advice, and most of the opportunities on this site started with a conversation like that.

  • Go to office hours or email to ask for 15 minutes. It's normal, even if you've never taken their class.
  • Ask for advice, not a favor: "What would you do in my position?" "Which classes or programs should I look at?" "Who else should I talk to?"
  • Ask about their research, and read one of their papers first.
  • Look outside your department. Sociology, economics, psychology and urban studies faculty take political science students too.
  • Follow up. Send a thank-you, then tell them what you did with their advice. That is how a professor becomes a recommender.

Sample email

Subject: Advice on research opportunities, [Your name], [major]

I'm a [year] majoring in [major] at Queens College. I read your [paper or project] on [topic], and I'm interested in [one sentence on why].

Pair your major

Take a broad range of courses early. Trying history, philosophy, economics or urban studies is how you find the questions you actually want to study, and a second field can make you a stronger applicant.

Theory, history and cities

Philosophy

Minor (18 credits) or second major

Arguments about justice, rights and legitimacy, the core of political theory. The minor includes two history-of-philosophy courses (PHIL 140–148). Best for students drawn to political theory.

History

Minor or second major

Primary sources, archives and long-run change. Useful for political theory, comparative politics and the history of American institutions.

Urban Studies

Major, or a BA and MA in five years

City politics, housing, policing and budgets, often with NYC data. The Accelerated MA pairs a BA in Urban Studies with an MA in Urban Affairs in five years, and up to 12 graduate credits count toward both. It needs a 3.0 GPA overall and in the major; majors in other social sciences are considered case by case. Apply at about 45–75 credits. Best for city government and MPA programs.

Sociology

Double major

Survey research and theory; home of Data Analytics. It unlocks the 4+1 BA/MA in data analytics and Mellon Mays, neither of which takes political science alone.

Data, economics and business

A quantitative credential helps for policy, campaign, government and research jobs. The Bureau of Labor Statistics projects data scientist jobs to grow 35% from 2025 to 2035 (BLS).

Data Analytics

Minor (21 credits), in Sociology

The easiest add-on for most political science majors, built for students with no background. Courses: DATA 205 (or PSYCH 107 or ECON 249), 212W, 235, 306, 333 and 334 (list). Best for analyst and research assistant jobs, predocs and MPP programs.

Math: Data Science and Statistics

BA, as a second major

Statistics, programming and modeling. Best for methods-heavy PhDs and data science jobs.

Economics

Major or minor

Policy analysis and econometrics. Best for MPPs, economics predocs and think tanks. For more advanced methods, the Quantitative Economics BS adds calculus, linear algebra, advanced econometrics and scientific computing, and is a STEM major.

Computer Science

Minors

Also Computer Information Technology. Best for computational social science and tech policy.

Text as data

Courses, a minor or a BS in Linguistics and Communication Disorders

Want to analyze speeches, bills or social media posts with code? The department teaches LCD 151 and 251, Methods in Computational Linguistics I and II. Go further with the minor (built for CS and linguistics majors; 33 credits for other majors) or the BS in Computational Linguistics.

Business and Liberal Arts (BALA)

Honors minor (21 credits), open to all majors

Business concepts, communication and analytical decision-making. Best for consulting, government management and MPA programs.

Skills employers look for, whatever you pair it with

  • A statistical language. R or Python, plus Excel. SQL if you want data jobs.
  • Survey and research design. Writing a questionnaire, sampling, reading a regression table.
  • Short-form writing. Policy memos and one-page briefs, not only seminar papers.
  • Proof. A GitHub page with one or two finished projects. See the samples.

Methods and skills

Queens College already teaches solid research methods in PSCI 200 and in intro statistics courses like PSYCH 107 and DATA 205. If you want advanced training, the funded summer research programs like RBSI and MIT MSRP teach graduate-level methods. They are just as good as a summer internship, if not better, and some pay more. Outside class, you already have free access to LinkedIn Learning.

Free self-study, in order

  1. OpenIntro Statistics. Probability, inference and regression from scratch (free PDF).
  2. R for Data Science. Cleaning, plotting and summarizing data in R.
  3. MIT 17.801, Political Science Scope and Methods. How political scientists design research.
  4. Blackwell, A User's Guide to Statistical Inference and Regression. Regression, done carefully. Good the summer before a PhD.
  5. Research Design in the Social Sciences and Causal Inference: The Remix. Causal inference, once regression feels comfortable.

Applying

Political science PhD applications are due from late November to December 17 for fall entry (Columbia's is Nov 25), and the APSA Diversity Fellowship closes earlier, on October 25, 2026.

Application fees ($75–$125 each) and who waives them

Program not on the list? Email the department's graduate coordinator and ask anyway, in October rather than the week of the deadline.

The GRE is back at most top programs

  • Required for fall 2027. Harvard (Government), Princeton, Stanford, Yale, Duke, Berkeley, Columbia, MIT, UChicago, UCLA.
  • Not required. Michigan, Wisconsin, Northwestern, CUNY Graduate Center.
  • Plan. Check each program page, and test by early fall of senior year.

The rest of the file

  • Letters (usually 3). From faculty who have seen your research or writing. Ask 6–8 weeks ahead with a one-page summary and your school list.
  • Statement of purpose. Lead with the question you want to study, show your preparation, and name 2–3 faculty per school whose work fits.
  • Writing sample. Your best research paper, often a seminar paper or thesis chapter, cut to the page limit and revised with a professor.
  • Where to apply. Fit with faculty matters more than rank. Look for people whose recent papers you would want to have written.

PhD visit programs

Many universities invite prospective PhD applicants to campus in the fall to meet faculty and graduate students and learn how to apply. Most pay for travel, a hotel and meals, and some waive the application fee. Most fly-ins take applications in July and August, so apply the summer before senior year.

Every visit program is in the PhD opportunities list. Open now: Notre Dame's Our Futures Weekend (juniors, due Nov 15) and Princeton's P3 (due Mar 15).

No visit? You can still get a fee waiver. Yale waives its fee for Pell Grant recipients, and UChicago Social Sciences waives it for undergrads on need-based aid. See Applying for more.

Tips

  • Numbers make bullets believable. "4,000+ respondents" beats "large dataset."
  • Tailor the top third to the job or program.
  • Keep a master CV and cut it down for each application.
  • Drop high school once you have college experience.
  • Send a PDF named LastName_Resume.pdf.
  • Get it reviewed at QC's Center for Career Engagement and Internships (Frese 213).
