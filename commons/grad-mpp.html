What an MPP is

A Master of Public Policy teaches you to analyze and evaluate policy with economics and statistics. It is faster than a PhD, but you usually pay for it.

  • Length: About 2 years
  • Cost: You usually pay. Some schools fund everyone: Princeton SPIA's MPA, a policy degree, covers full tuition for every student
  • At CUNY: Baruch's Marxe School starts an MPP in fall 2027: evening classes, in person and hybrid, 36–39 credits, about 2 years full-time. Tuition is $545 a credit for New York residents, pending CUNY Board approval
  • Pick it if: You want to design policy or judge whether programs work
  • Prepare: Take economics and statistics. For a future MPP or MPA student, the most valuable program is the PPIA Junior Summer Institute

MPP or MPA? Schools use the two labels loosely, so read each program's courses, not just its name. An MPP leans on analysis; an MPA leans on management.

MPP timeline

Junior year is the busiest stretch for policy students: PPIA, the Albany session internships, congressional internships and Truman all close between October and February.

Sophomores, juniors and seniors · CUNY · government internships for credit

Serve in the office of a city, state or federal legislator or a government agency while taking a seminar with faculty. You can also find your own placement with your campus coordinator. The program recruits for the Albany session programs too.

  • Who: On most campuses, juniors, seniors and highly qualified sophomores, any major. Some campuses require an intro political science or public administration course
  • Credit: 3 to 12 credits a semester, depending on the campus and program
  • Pay: Some placements, like the Caucus CUNY Scholars Program, come with a scholarship, and the Albany legislative internships pay stipends
  • Start: Ask the Political Science department who coordinates it at Queens College

Journalism minor (Media Studies)

When: Anytime · declare it with the department
Opens: Anytime

All years · Queens College · 18 credits

Reporting, writing, editing and digital journalism, taught by people with newsroom experience. It pairs well with political science: PSCI 217 and 220 are on the elective list, though courses that count for your major can't also count here.

  • Required: JOURN 100 (Beat the Press), 101W (Introduction to News Reporting), 200W (Reporting from the Field) and 201 (Digital Journalism)
  • Electives: Two of JOURN 202, 265, 300 (Internship in Journalism), 304 and 310, or approved courses in other departments

NYC Public Service Corps (Federal Work-Study)

When: Fall–spring · placements end Mar 5, 2027

Sophomores, juniors and seniors · Paid internship through your work-study award · QC Financial Aid

A paid internship that uses your Federal Work-Study grant. Work-study students can also find on-campus and off-campus jobs on HireQC (Handshake).

  • Who: Students with a Federal Work-Study award: file the FAFSA (QC code 002690) and accept the award in CUNYfirst. Open to US citizens, permanent residents and other eligible noncitizens
  • Pay: $18 an hour on campus and $19 off campus for undergraduates
  • How: Complete the Work Study Internship Application with your résumé. Funds are limited; questions to qcworkstudy@qc.cuny.edu

Political Science department awards

When: No application · the department chooses

Juniors and seniors · Political Science department · QC

The department gives named awards to its own students, including the Joel S. Morrison Scholarship ($500 to a senior graduating with honors, with preference for students headed to law school), the Andrew Hacker Scholarship (academic excellence and financial need; preference for first-generation students), the E. Hevesi Memorial Award ($250 for academic excellence and commitment to the political process), the George Priestley Memorial Scholarship (Latin America and the Caribbean) and the Hratch Zadoian Human Rights Essay Prize.

  • How: Most have no application: do strong work and get to know your professors. Questions: the department, 718-997-5470

QC Internship Stipend Fund

When: Rolling, 2026 · –27 applications open since September

All years · Center for Career Engagement and Internships · Frese Hall 213

A one-time $2,700 award for an unpaid internship or unpaid research: 150 hours over 5 to 10 weeks, in spring, summer or fall. It is meant for fields that don't usually pay interns, like public service, nonprofits, education and the arts.

  • How: A Microsoft Form with a QC faculty or staff recommender, a personal statement and a résumé. Questions: stipend@qc.cuny.edu
  • Award: $2,700, once

The New York Times Corps

When: Spring · applications open each spring
Opens: Spring 2027

Freshmen and sophomores · Remote · mentoring until you graduate

A Times journalist mentors you through college, at least three times a year. About 20 students a year, with preference for low-income students and colleges that rarely send people to the Times. Strong members can be considered for the Times Fellowship.

  • Who: Rising sophomores and juniors (apply as a freshman or sophomore). International students at US colleges can apply
  • Pay: Unpaid mentoring; members who finish can earn a paid trip to the newsroom

Deadline: Oct 26, 2026 · then Feb 1 and Mar 8

A paid AmeriCorps year tutoring and mentoring students in NYC elementary and middle schools. Full-year spots for 2027–28 start in summer 2027.
