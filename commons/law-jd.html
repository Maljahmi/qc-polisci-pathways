What law school takes

Admissions weigh two numbers most, your LSAT score and your GPA, then your personal statement, letters and résumé. Your major does not need to be pre-law or political science.

One application cycle (LSAC's steps)

  1. Spring before you apply. Request the LSAC fee waiver if you might qualify, at least 6 months before your first LSAT.
  2. June or September–October. Take the LSAT. Many schools want a score by November or December.
  3. Summer. Draft the personal statement; ask 2–3 professors or supervisors for letters.
  4. 6–8 weeks before your first deadline. Register for CAS, which sends your transcripts and letters to every school.
  5. September–December. Submit applications. Earlier is better at schools that admit on a rolling basis.
  6. Winter–spring. Decisions and scholarship offers. QC's pre-law advisor helps you compare and negotiate.

The LSAT today

Since August 2024 there are no logic games: two scored Logical Reasoning sections, one scored Reading Comprehension section and one unscored section, scored 120–180, plus a separate Argumentative Writing sample (LSAC). Many schools also accept the GRE.

Time off is normal

  • 86% of Harvard Law's Class of 2029 spent at least a year out of college (HLS)
  • ~1 in 3 of 2024 first-year law students nationally came straight from college (LSAC)

Your timeline

Freshman and sophomore years are for GPA, professors and a first internship. The LSAT comes junior summer at the earliest.

QC Scholarships, General Application

Deadline: Oct 25, 2026 · spring cycle mid-Jan to mid-Mar

All years · QC Foundation scholarships · one application, every semester

One general application on QC's AcademicWorks portal puts you in the running for most of the college's donor scholarships, based on merit, need, major or class year. Fall applications pay for the spring term, and spring applications pay for the fall. A few awards add a short supplemental question at the end.

  • Who: Matriculated QC students with a QC email, including second-degree students. The office says most awards are open to students without documentation
  • For political science: Minister Jason Peck ($500; preference for political science majors and minors), Sig and Mary Hecht (financial need and a 3.0+ GPA in a social science), Swimmer (preference for students interested in law) and Brownstein/McDermott ($5,000; political theory counts; 30+ credits and a 3.5+ GPA)
  • Tip: Apply every semester. The office suggests listing a reference (email them first) and any community service

CUNY Edward T. Rogowsky Internship Program in Government and Public Affairs

When: Each semester · through a campus faculty coordinator

Sophomores, juniors and seniors · CUNY · government internships for credit

Serve in the office of a city, state or federal legislator or a government agency while taking a seminar with faculty. You can also find your own placement with your campus coordinator. The program recruits for the Albany session programs too.

  • Who: On most campuses, juniors, seniors and highly qualified sophomores, any major. Some campuses require an intro political science or public administration course

  • Pay: Some placements, like the Caucus CUNY Scholars Program, come with a scholarship, and the Albany legislative internships pay stipends
  • Start: Ask the Political Science department who coordinates it at Queens College
