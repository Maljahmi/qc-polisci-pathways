Start here

This path collects the free pre-law programs, NYC internships, scholarships and LSAT logistics a QC political science major needs, whether you apply senior year or five years from now.

Three ground rules

  • The best pre-law programs are free. The LSAC fee waiver, LSAC Plus and most pipeline programs cost nothing. Be skeptical of paid "pre-law institutes."
  • Deadlines move every year. Confirm on the official page before you plan around a date.
  • Taking time off is normal. Most law students did not go straight from college.

Fields of law

Law is many different jobs. You don't have to pick a field before law school, but knowing them helps you choose internships and explain your interest in your personal statement. For a paragraph on each, see LSAC's Fields of Law; for more depth, read the guide linked under each field below. Most were written by law school career offices.

  • Criminal law. Prosecutors charge and try crimes for the government; defense lawyers, often public defenders, represent the people accused. Read: UCLA Law's guides to public defense and prosecution. Try it: the Queens DA or the city's defender offices.
  • Civil rights and civil liberties. Lawyers who use lawsuits, policy advocacy and government enforcement to protect equal treatment, voting, speech and other rights. Read: Civil Rights/Civil Liberties (Georgetown Law). Try it: the ACLU, LatinoJustice LAWbound or the Justice Department's Civil Rights Division.
  • Immigration law. Lawyers who help people get visas, green cards, citizenship or asylum, and defend people facing deportation. A second language helps. Read: Immigration Law Career Guide (Harvard Law). Try it: Immigrant Justice Corps after college.
  • Public interest and legal aid. Nonprofit lawyers who represent low-income clients for free in problems like evictions, debt, public benefits and consumer fraud. Read: Legal Services Career Guide (Harvard Law, 2013). Try it: the Hope Reichbach Fellowship at a Brooklyn nonprofit.
  • Government. City, state and federal lawyers who advise agencies, enforce and defend laws in court, and draft legislation and regulations. Read: State and Local Government Career Guide (Harvard Law), which profiles the NYC Law Department. Try it: the State Attorney General or the Mayor's Office.
  • Corporate and business law. Lawyers who help companies form, raise money, merge and make deals, mostly drafting and negotiating contracts rather than going to court. Most start at large firms. Read: Careers in Transactional Law (Georgetown Law, 2021). Try it: the SEO Law Fellowship.
  • Civil litigation. Lawyers who handle non-criminal lawsuits between people, companies or governments, from gathering evidence through settlement, trial and appeal. Read: Civil Litigation (University of Baltimore Law). Try it: a judge's chambers through the Sotomayor internship or the state courts.
  • International law and human rights. Lawyers working on the rules between countries and on human rights, for governments, the UN, international courts, nonprofits or global firms. Read: Careers in Public International Law and Human Rights (UCLA Law, 2019). Try it: the Council on Foreign Relations or, after college, the Carnegie Junior Fellows.
  • Environmental and energy law. Lawyers working on pollution, climate, land, water and energy rules for agencies, advocacy groups, companies or utilities. Read: Different Career Paths in Environmental Law (American Bar Association, 2020). Try it: the Udall Scholarship.
  • Labor and employment law. Lawyers who handle unions, wages, discrimination and firing, for workers, unions, employers or agencies like the NLRB and EEOC. Read: Employment/Labor Law (Georgetown Law).
  • Family law. Lawyers who handle divorce, custody, child support, adoption, domestic violence and child-welfare cases. Read: Constructing a Legal Education for a Career in Family Law (NYU Law), which names New York City employers. Try it: the city's defender offices, several of which have family defense practices.
  • Intellectual property, technology and privacy. Lawyers who protect inventions, brands and creative work, and advise on technology, data privacy and cybersecurity. Patent work needs a science or engineering background; trademark, copyright and privacy work doesn't. Read: Intellectual Property Law (Georgetown Law).
  • Health law. Lawyers who advise hospitals, insurers, drug companies, patients and agencies on health regulation, fraud, privacy and access to care. Read: Health Law (Georgetown Law).
  • Tax law. Lawyers who plan deals around tax rules and handle audits and disputes, at firms, accounting firms, companies or the government. Read: An Introduction to Tax Careers for J.D.s (American Bar Association, 2019).
  • Election law and voting rights. Lawyers who work on voting rights, redistricting, campaign finance and how elections are run, for campaigns, parties, nonprofits and election agencies. Close to political science. Read: Polling Election Lawyers (Harvard Law's Center on the Legal Profession, 2022). Try it: the Brennan Center or Emgage in Queens.

QC and CUNY resources

Start with QC's pre-law advisor, who sits in the Academic Advising Center, and the Legal Studies minor, whose three required courses also count toward the major.

  • Clubs. The pre-law page mentions a Legal Studies Club that meets monthly. The QC Pre-Law Society, Mock Trial, Women in Law and Model UN are listed on UKnighted, and PSCI 288 covers mock trial and moot court. More in Getting involved at Queens College.
  • A warning about research programs. The CUNY Pipeline Program, Leadership Alliance and Big Ten SROP exclude students headed to law school.
  • Scholarships. Fill out QC's General Application every semester; one award in it, the Swimmer Scholarship, gives preference to students interested in law. The Political Science department's Morrison Scholarship favors seniors headed to law school, and graduating seniors with a 3.75+ GPA are considered for law fellowships at the Baccalaureate ceremony. Muslim seniors headed to law school can apply to the Islamic Scholarship Fund's law track. More in Scholarships and funding.
  • Government and nonprofit work. Paid internships with the State Attorney General, the Mayor's Office, the Supreme Court and the ACLU, plus more in Journalism, nonprofits and government.
  • Jobs, letters and networking. HireQC (Handshake), résumé help, free certificates, job boards and CUNY's AI tool are in Job search tools and AI. See also Recommendation letters and Building a network.

Research as a pre-law student

Research isn't required for law school, but it builds what admissions reads for: careful writing and a professor who knows your work.

  • Honors in the Social Sciences. A thesis on bail, sentencing or voting rights gives you a writing sample and a close mentor. 3.3 GPA to start.
  • Methods courses. PSCI 200 and an intro statistics course, such as PSYCH 107 or DATA 205, teach you to read the empirical studies courts and advocates cite.
  • Faculty research work. Email a professor whose work on courts, policing, immigration or rights interests you, and offer a set number of hours a week.
  • Data work at legal organizations. Some defender offices, DA offices and legal aid groups take interns who clean data and summarize case records.
  • Present it. The Borough of Queens Undergraduate Research Symposium takes projects from every field each spring.
  • Split between law and a PhD? Research serves both. See the graduate school path for PhD programs.

LSAT and fees

One LSAT plus CAS and six school reports costs about $740 before prep. If money is tight, apply for the LSAC fee waiver at least six months before your first test.

2026–27 test dates (LSAC)

What it costs (LSAC)

The LSAC fee waiver. Based on income and assets. Income limits run from 150% to 300% of the federal poverty guidelines, depending on the tier and whether your parents' income counts. Tier 1 covers 2 LSATs with Writing and Score Preview, CAS, 6 law school reports and LawHub Advantage; Tier 2 covers 1 LSAT, CAS, 3 reports and LawHub Advantage. The LSAT and CAS benefits last two years; LawHub Advantage lasts one year. US citizens, permanent residents, DACA recipients, asylum seekers and TPS holders are eligible. Apply, and ask each law school whether it waives its own fee too.

Free and low-cost prep

  • LawHub. The free tier includes full official practice tests. Khan Academy's free LSAT course ended June 30, 2024; its content moved here.
  • LawHub Advantage. The paid tier with many more official tests, included with LSAC Plus and the fee waiver.
  • AccessLex LexPreLaw. Kaplan LSAT prep, admissions counseling and financial help for 150 underrepresented applicants a year. You need an LSAT score already, at or below the 25th percentile. The 2027 application opens in March.
  • The GRE option. ETS's list includes NYU, Columbia, Fordham, Brooklyn, Cardozo, St. John's, Pace, Hofstra and Touro. CUNY Law is not on it.

Gap years

A paid job between college and law school is the norm at top schools, and the work you do can become the story in your personal statement.

Using the time well. Keep in touch with one or two professors so they can still write letters. Take the LSAT while you are working rather than cramming in the fall you apply.

Sample law school résumé

A law school résumé is a job résumé with three differences: list your hours per week for jobs held during school, add an interests line, and skip the objective.

Download the samples

Open the Word file to edit it, or the PDF to print. Replace everything with your own details.

  • Law résumé: Word · PDF

Rules from admissions offices

  • 1–2 pages. Trim to one if the second page is less than half full (Maryland Carey Law).
  • One page where asked. Wisconsin's advisors name Yale and Northwestern (UW–Madison pre-law).
  • Hours per week for every job held during school. It explains a GPA earned while working.
  • An interests line brings the application to life.
  • No high school, no objective, no fancy fonts.

Before you send it

  • Make every date match your application form.
  • Keep one format: title, organization, city, dates, hours.
  • Ask QC Pre-Law Advising to review it.
