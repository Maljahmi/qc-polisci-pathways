Grad school: PhD /grad-phd/

Overview · PhD · MPP · MPA · MA · Guides

What a PhD is

Original research and teaching. A PhD prepares you to be a professor, or to do research at think tanks, in government and in tech. Political science PhD programs pay you.

  • Length: 5–6 years
  • Who pays: The school: tuition plus a stipend. 2026–27: Princeton $52,680, Duke $43,775, CUNY Graduate Center $31,141
  • Pick it if: You want answering questions with data or theory to be your career
  • Apply: Fall of senior year or later. Most deadlines fall Dec 1–17; Columbia's is Nov 25. See Applying

A political science PhD can also lead to data science. PhD students train in statistics, causal inference, survey design and coding, and some go straight into industry. Michigan's placement records include a research scientist on Meta's demography and survey science team, a director of data science at Civiqs and a lead data scientist at Cox Automotive. MIT's include a data scientist at NORC. Choosing methodology as a minor field keeps that door open.

A master's is not required for a PhD. Most political science PhD students are admitted straight from a BA or after a paid research job. A paid year or two first is normal; the standard route is a predoc, a full-time research assistant job.

Where to look. predoc.org is the main job board; filter by political economy or public policy. Listings go stale, so confirm on the lab's own page. For a pay benchmark, Stanford's SIEPR economics predoc posted $64,260 a year.

Find your subfield

You don't have to be a "methods person" to get a political science PhD. You apply to study one subfield, only some subfields are math-heavy, and most programs teach statistics from the start.

The five core subfields

American politics

Math: Mixed · often quantitative
Also called American government and politics

U.S. institutions such as Congress, the presidency and the courts, plus elections, public opinion and political behavior.

  • Look for: No special prerequisites are listed. Expect statistics: Harvard's math prefresher notes it is hard to take American politics courses without meeting statistical or game-theoretic models

Comparative politics

Math: Mixed · qualitative and quantitative

Compares politics across countries and regions: democracy, development, civil war, institutions and identity.

  • Look for: A foreign language and knowledge of a region. Rutgers tests majors in the language they need, and Michigan advises starting languages before grad school
  • Research: Berkeley PhD students usually spend at least a year doing fieldwork

International relations

Math: Mixed · depends on approach
Also called international relations and world politics

War and security, trade, international law and organizations, and why countries cooperate or fight.

  • Look for: Methods vary: historical, statistical and game-theoretic work all appear. No IR-specific prerequisites are listed

Political theory

Math: Light · little math
Also called political thought, or normative political theory and philosophy

Ideas of justice, legitimacy, democracy and power, read through the history of political thought and contemporary philosophy.

  • Look for: Reading in philosophy, history or classics, and often a foreign language: UVA requires one for theory majors
  • Methods: Duke's methods requirements don't apply to theory students, Harvard lets them skip its methods course, and Berkeley weighs math less for theory applicants

Methodology and formal theory

Math: Heavy · real math
Also called research methods, quantitative methodology, or formal and quantitative analysis

Methodologists build the statistical and research-design tools other political scientists use. Formal theorists use game-theory models of strategic choices.

  • Look for: Math and statistics. Duke's methodology field expects competency in both, and Michigan asks methods majors for math coursework
  • Tip: Many students take methods as a minor next to a substantive subfield

You don't have to arrive methods-heavy

  • Math camps. Harvard, Stanford, Michigan, Princeton and UC San Diego run summer math courses for incoming PhD students. At Harvard, almost every first-year student takes it.
  • Methods from scratch. Stanford says you don't need advanced quantitative courses to be admitted, and its first-year sequence assumes no background in statistics or advanced math.
  • Language or methods. At the CUNY Graduate Center, UCLA, Yale and Columbia, a research-tools requirement can be met with a foreign language or with methods courses.
  • Your topic can change. Michigan notes that students often change focus once coursework starts. The statement of purpose is not a contract.

Be honest with yourself about math. Quantitative skills still count. Most top programs require the GRE again, and Stanford reports admitted students typically score 163 or higher on the quantitative section. Stanford also requires two methods courses of every PhD student. Taking PSCI 200, PSCI 275 and a statistics course at QC helps whichever subfield you choose.

Other fields at some schools

Some departments add fields beyond the core five. Check whether a field can be your main field or only a minor.

  • Public policy. CUNY Graduate Center (Public Policy, with areas like health, education, housing and urban development), UC Berkeley (Public Policy & Organization), UT Austin, Texas A&M (Public Policy / Public Administration), Syracuse (public administration and policy) and Michigan (a student-designed Public Policy and Administration minor).
  • Law and courts. Princeton and Rutgers (Public Law), Michigan (Law, Courts, and Politics), UC Berkeley (Public Law & Jurisprudence), Washington and UT Austin, which also has a combined JD/PhD.
  • Race, ethnicity and gender. UCLA (Race, Ethnicity & Politics), Rutgers (Women and Politics), UC Berkeley, Washington and Ohio State (Race, Ethnicity, and Gender).
  • Political psychology and behavior. Stony Brook, Ohio State and Minnesota (as a minor).
  • Political economy and formal theory. Rochester (formal political theory; every student takes it or methodology), Yale, NYU and UChicago, which runs a separate Political Economy PhD with its public policy school. Expect heavy math.
  • Organized by theme. Duke has no American, comparative or international relations fields. Its six fields are Political Behavior & Identities, Political Institutions, Political Economy, Security, Peace & Conflict, Political Methodology, and Normative Political Theory & Philosophy.

Grad school: MPP /grad-mpp/

Overview · PhD · MPP · MPA · MA · Guides

What an MPP is

A Master of Public Policy teaches you to analyze and evaluate policy with economics and statistics. It is faster than a PhD, but you usually pay for it.

  • Length: About 2 years
  • Cost: You usually pay. Some schools fund everyone: Princeton SPIA's MPA, a policy degree, covers full tuition for every student
  • Pick it if: You want to design policy or judge whether programs work
  • Prepare: Take economics and statistics. For a future MPP or MPA student, the most valuable program is the PPIA Junior Summer Institute

MPP or MPA? Schools use the two labels loosely, so read each program's courses, not just its name. An MPP leans on analysis; an MPA leans on management.

Grad school: MPA /grad-mpa/

Overview · PhD · MPP · MPA · MA · Guides

What an MPA is

A Master of Public Administration teaches you to manage public and nonprofit organizations: budgets, operations and people. CUNY's MPA programs cost far less than private ones.

  • Length: 1–2 years
  • Cost: Baruch Marxe and John Jay charge NY residents $6,375 a semester full-time; Columbia SIPA tuition is $74,220 a year
  • Pick it if: You want to run programs, agencies or nonprofits
  • Prepare: City, state and nonprofit internships, then fellowships like Coro and the CUNY Civil Service Pathways Fellowship

Your timeline

Future MPA students build a record of public service: city, state and nonprofit work, then a fellowship or a city job.

Grad school: MA /grad-ma/

Overview · PhD · MPP · MPA · MA · Guides

What an MA is

A master's in political science gives you graduate-level political science and methods without committing to a PhD. It helps if your record needs strengthening first. A master's in methods or data is another route.

  • Length: 1–2 years
  • CUNY: Graduate Center (30 credits, master's thesis); Brooklyn College (30 credits, evening and online); City College offers a Master of International Affairs instead
  • Private: UChicago MAPSS (one year; 73% of graduates who applied to PhDs got funded offers) and Columbia cost far more
  • Methods or data: QC's own Data Analytics programs lead to an MA, with a faster 4+1 route for sociology majors. The Columbia SPS CUNY Fellowship fully funds some Columbia master's for CUNY students
  • Pick it if: You need more methods before a PhD, or want a data career now

Your timeline

An MA works as a bridge to a PhD or a data career. The QC route is the 4+1, which needs a sociology major and a 3.5 GPA by junior spring.

Opportunities

Grad school: Guides /grad-guides/

Overview · PhD · MPP · MPA · MA · Guides

Start here

This path covers funded PhDs and policy master's for any Queens College Political Science major who is even curious about grad school. You don't need to be sure yet. Most of these programs exist to help you find out.

Three ground rules

  • Good research programs pay you. Almost everything here is free and comes with a stipend, housing, or both. Be skeptical of programs that charge you to "do research."
  • Deadlines move every year. Confirm on the official page before you plan around a date.
  • Apply to more than one. These programs are competitive, and a rejection says little about you.
  • Take the methods courses early. PSCI 200 (Research Skills) and PSCI 275 (Applied Political Research), per the QC catalog.
  • Ask for research assistant work. No office posts it. Pick one or two professors whose work you understand, and email what you read, what skill you bring and how many hours you have.

Get credit for it. QC's political science internship courses give credit for urban politics, law and advocacy, and international politics placements. CUNY's Rogowsky Internship Program places juniors and seniors in government for 3–12 credits.

Undocumented or an international student? Emory SOAR says it accepts undocumented and international students. MIT MSRP accepts international students with a visa and work authorization. RBSI, MIT and Michigan also take DACA recipients.

Statements, help and AI

Pick programs that fit, and get help with your statement. Read what each program focuses on and make sure it matches what you want to study. Then ask your professors and Rebecca Baron, QC's Advisor for Scholarships and Fellowships in the Office of Honors and Scholarships, to help with your statements of purpose. They are here to help. Don't lose out on an opportunity because you were scared to ask. You're in college to learn, so learn.

Don't let AI write your application. AI is useful for some tasks, like building this website. But professors read the applications to these programs, and they will know if AI wrote yours. Use it as a proofreader instead: give it the prompt and your own draft, and ask where you could improve or show more of yourself. It's a machine, and it doesn't know you better than you know yourself. Before you submit, have peers and professors read your writing too.

Talk to your professors

Your research doesn't have to be political science. Methods travel across fields, and CUNY faculty in many departments need student help with surveys, data, experiments, fieldwork and archives.

What research looks like next door

  • Sociology. Surveys and interviews on inequality, immigration, work and neighborhoods. Home of QC's data analytics program.
  • Economics. Labor markets and policy evaluation, often with large administrative datasets and causal inference.
  • Psychology. Experiments on attitudes, prejudice and decision-making, the base of political psychology.
  • Urban studies. Housing, transit, policing and city budgets, often with NYC open data.
  • Anthropology and history. Fieldwork, oral histories and archives.
  • Linguistics. Text as data, and language and identity (see Pair your major).

Talk to your professors. They're there for you.

Office hours are not only for grade questions. Professors expect students to come in and ask for advice, and most of the opportunities on this site started with a conversation like that.

  • Go to office hours or email to ask for 15 minutes. It's normal, even if you've never taken their class.
  • Ask for advice, not a favor: "What would you do in my position?" "Which classes or programs should I look at?" "Who else should I talk to?"
  • Ask about their research, and read one of their papers first.
  • Look outside your department. Sociology, economics, psychology and urban studies faculty take political science students too.
  • Follow up. Send a thank-you, then tell them what you did with their advice. That is how a professor becomes a recommender.

Applying

Political science PhD applications are due from late November to December 17 for fall entry (Columbia's is Nov 25), and the APSA Diversity Fellowship closes earlier, on October 25, 2026.

Application fees ($75–$125 each) and who waives them

Program not on the list? Email the department's graduate coordinator and ask anyway, in October rather than the week of the deadline.

The GRE is back at most top programs

  • Required for fall 2027. Harvard (Government), Princeton, Stanford, Yale, Duke, Berkeley, Columbia, MIT, UChicago, UCLA.
  • Not required. Michigan, Wisconsin, Northwestern, CUNY Graduate Center.
  • Plan. Check each program page, and test by early fall of senior year.

The rest of the file

  • Letters (usually 3). From faculty who have seen your research or writing. Ask 6–8 weeks ahead with a one-page summary and your school list.
  • Statement of purpose. Lead with the question you want to study, show your preparation, and name 2–3 faculty per school whose work fits.
  • Writing sample. Your best research paper, often a seminar paper or thesis chapter, cut to the page limit and revised with a professor.
  • Where to apply. Fit with faculty matters more than rank. Look for people whose recent papers you would want to have written.

Many universities invite prospective PhD applicants to campus in the fall to meet faculty and graduate students and learn how to apply. Most pay for travel, a hotel and meals, and some waive the application fee. Most fly-ins take applications in July and August, so apply the summer before senior year.

Every visit program is in the PhD opportunities list. Open now: Notre Dame's Our Futures Weekend (juniors, due Nov 15) and Princeton's P3 (due Mar 15).

Law school: JD /law-jd/

Overview · JD · Guides

What law school takes

Admissions weigh two numbers most, your LSAT score and your GPA, then your personal statement, letters and résumé. Your major does not need to be pre-law or political science.

One application cycle (LSAC's steps)

  1. Spring before you apply. Request the LSAC fee waiver if you might qualify, at least 6 months before your first LSAT.
  2. June or September–October. Take the LSAT. Many schools want a score by November or December.
  3. Summer. Draft the personal statement; ask 2–3 professors or supervisors for letters.
  4. 6–8 weeks before your first deadline. Register for CAS, which sends your transcripts and letters to every school.
  5. September–December. Submit applications. Earlier is better at schools that admit on a rolling basis.
  6. Winter–spring. Decisions and scholarship offers. QC's pre-law advisor helps you compare and negotiate.

The LSAT today

Since August 2024 there are no logic games: two scored Logical Reasoning sections, one scored Reading Comprehension section and one unscored section, scored 120–180, plus a separate Argumentative Writing sample (LSAC). Many schools also accept the GRE.

Time off is normal

  • 86% of Harvard Law's Class of 2029 spent at least a year out of college (HLS)
  • 1 in 3 2024 first-year law students nationally came straight from college, about (LSAC)

Your timeline

Freshman and sophomore years are for GPA, professors and a first internship. The LSAT comes junior summer at the earliest.

Law school: Guides /law-guides/

Overview · JD · Guides

Start here

This path collects the free pre-law programs, NYC internships, scholarships and LSAT logistics a QC political science major needs, whether you apply senior year or five years from now.

Three ground rules

  • The best pre-law programs are free. The LSAC fee waiver, LSAC Plus and most pipeline programs cost nothing. Be skeptical of paid "pre-law institutes."
  • Deadlines move every year. Confirm on the official page before you plan around a date.
  • Taking time off is normal. Most law students did not go straight from college.

QC and CUNY resources

Start with QC's pre-law advisor, who sits in the Academic Advising Center, and the Legal Studies minor, whose three required courses also count toward the major.

  • Clubs. The pre-law page mentions a Legal Studies Club that meets monthly. Model UN, the Political Science Club and QC Future 1Ls are listed on UKnighted. The Political Science Club lists mock trials among its activities, and PSCI 288 covers mock trial and moot court.
  • A warning about research programs. The CUNY Pipeline Program, Leadership Alliance and Big Ten SROP exclude students headed to law school.

Research as a pre-law student

Research isn't required for law school, but it builds what admissions reads for: careful writing and a professor who knows your work.

  • Honors in the Social Sciences. A thesis on bail, sentencing or voting rights gives you a writing sample and a close mentor. 3.3 GPA to start.
  • Methods courses. PSCI 200 and PSCI 275 teach you to read the empirical studies courts and advocates cite.
  • Faculty research work. Email a professor whose work on courts, policing, immigration or rights interests you, and offer a set number of hours a week.
  • Data work at legal organizations. Some defender offices, DA offices and legal aid groups take interns who clean data and summarize case records.
  • Present it. The Borough of Queens Undergraduate Research Symposium takes projects from every field each spring.
  • Split between law and a PhD?. Research serves both. See the grad school path for PhD programs.

LSAT and fees

One LSAT plus CAS and six school reports costs about $740 before prep. If money is tight, apply for the LSAC fee waiver at least six months before your first test.

What it costs (LSAC)

The LSAC fee waiver Based on income and assets. Income limits run from 150% to 300% of the federal poverty guidelines, depending on the tier and whether your parents' income counts. Tier 1 covers 2 LSATs with Writing and Score Preview, CAS, 6 law school reports and LawHub Advantage; Tier 2 covers 1 LSAT, CAS, 3 reports and LawHub Advantage. The LSAT and CAS benefits last two years; LawHub Advantage lasts one year. US citizens, permanent residents, DACA recipients, asylum seekers and TPS holders are eligible. Apply, and ask each law school whether it waives its own fee too.

Free and low-cost prep

  • LawHub. The free tier includes full official practice tests. Khan Academy's free LSAT course ended June 30, 2024; its content moved here.
  • LawHub Advantage. The paid tier with many more official tests, included with LSAC Plus and the fee waiver.
  • AccessLex LexPreLaw. Kaplan LSAT prep, admissions counseling and financial help for 150 underrepresented applicants a year. You need an LSAT score already, at or below the 25th percentile. The 2027 application opens in March.
  • The GRE option. ETS's list includes NYU, Columbia, Fordham, Brooklyn, Cardozo, St. John's, Pace, Hofstra and Touro. CUNY Law is not on it.

Gap years

A paid job between college and law school is the norm at top schools, and the work you do can become the story in your personal statement.

Using the time well. Keep in touch with one or two professors so they can still write letters. Take the LSAT while you are working rather than cramming in the fall you apply.

Rules from admissions offices

  • 1–2 pages. Trim to one if the second page is less than half full (Maryland Carey Law).
  • One page where asked. Wisconsin's advisors name Yale and Northwestern (UW–Madison pre-law).
  • Hours per week for every job held during school. It explains a GPA earned while working.
  • An interests line brings the application to life.
  • No high school, no objective, no fancy fonts.

Before you send it

  • Make every date match your application form.
  • Keep one format: title, organization, city, dates, hours.
  • Ask QC Pre-Law Advising to review it.
