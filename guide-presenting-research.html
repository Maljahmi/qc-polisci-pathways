Presenting and publishing research

Undergraduate conferences and journals that take student papers. Part of Get ready, for graduate school and law school.

Presenting a paper or poster shows graduate programs that you can do research, and it gives you a reason to turn a good class paper into a great one. Start with your best paper, revise it with the professor who assigned it, and send it out.

Get a paper ready

  • Pick one paper. Your strongest research paper, usually from a seminar or an honors thesis. Ask the professor to read a revision; some conferences require a faculty sponsor anyway.
  • Write an abstract. Usually 150 to 500 words, depending on the call: your question, how you answered it and what you found.
  • List it. Put presentations and publications on your CV. See the sample résumé and CV.

At Queens College and CUNY

  • Queens-Wide Undergraduate Research Symposium. A spring poster session with York, LaGuardia and Queensborough; 2026's was April 30. Details
  • CUNY Celebration of Undergraduate Research. CUNY's showcase in late May, by nomination from QC. Details
  • Money to travel. Ask the Office of Undergraduate Research (OUGR@qc.cuny.edu) and your department about help paying for a conference.

Conferences that take undergraduates

  • Pi Sigma Alpha National Student Research Conference. February 26–27, 2027, in Washington; proposals due October 31, 2026. Members only. Details
  • Midwest Political Science Association. Undergraduate poster sessions in Chicago, April 1–4, 2027; proposals due December 1, 2026, and you don't need to be a member. Details
  • National Conference on Undergraduate Research. Every field; Grand Rapids, April 12–14, 2027; abstracts due December 4, 2026. Details
  • Closer to home. The New York State Political Science Association has an undergraduate section (you need a faculty sponsor), and the Northeastern Political Science Association encourages undergraduates to submit.

Journals that publish undergraduates

  • Pi Sigma Alpha Undergraduate Journal of Politics. Any undergraduate, any major; due September 15 and March 5; up to 35 pages with a 150-word abstract, in APSA style. Submissions
  • Journal of Politics & International Affairs (Ohio State). Any undergraduate; papers of 10 to 30 pages; the last deadline was January 31. Guidelines
  • Michigan Journal of Political Science. Undergraduates only; 4,000 to 18,000 words, Chicago style. Submissions
  • Yale Review of International Studies. Undergraduates anywhere; 2,000 to 8,000 words for the spring issue, due February 19, 2027, and shorter online pieces any time. Guidelines
  • Politikon. The International Association for Political Science Students' open-access journal takes papers from students at every level, with no fees. About Politikon

Journals and conferences change their rules every year. Read the current call before you submit.
